title: "The EU Deforestation Regulation: a third delay, but a line drawn under further changes"
date: 2026-08-15
topic: Environment
tldr: "The EUDR has been postponed twice since 2023 and now applies from 30 December 2026, but the Commission's May 2026 simplification review explicitly refuses to reopen the law itself — offering guidance and narrower product-scope tweaks instead."
where_things_stand: >
  The EU Deforestation Regulation entered into force in June 2023, requiring due-diligence statements and plot-level geolocation traceability for cattle, cocoa, coffee, palm oil, rubber, soya and wood before they reach the EU market. Its application date has been postponed twice — first from December 2024 to December 2025, then again to 30 December 2026 (30 June 2027 for micro and small operators) — largely because the Commission's own due-diligence Information System was not judged ready. As mandated when the second delay was agreed, the Commission published a formal simplification review on 4 May 2026, alongside updated guidance, updated FAQs, and a draft delegated act narrowing the list of covered products. A finalised package, including the delegated act and an implementing act on the Information System, was adopted on 13 July 2026.
what_is_at_stake: >
  The headline of the May 2026 review is what it does not do: despite being explicitly tasked with assessing whether to reopen the Regulation, the Commission chose not to touch the core due-diligence obligations, instead delivering an estimated 75% reduction in annual compliance costs through guidance, a new lighter-touch "downstream operator" category, and narrow Annex I product adjustments (adding soluble coffee and certain palm oil derivatives; removing items such as leather and retreaded tyres). Some of the largest companies already in scope — Nestlé, Ferrero, Tony's Chocolonely and Barry Callebaut among them — publicly opposed further delay, saying they were ready to comply on the original schedule. The United States has separately pushed, without success so far, for producers in "negligible risk" countries to be exempted from due diligence altogether.
why_it_matters: >
  For large operators and traders in the seven covered commodities, the third application deadline in a row is both a relief and a credibility test: further slippage would undermine confidence that the Commission can actually deliver a working compliance system. For downstream retailers and manufacturers, the new notification-only regime for non-primary actors meaningfully lowers the compliance bar. For micro and small producers, including farmers and foresters, the longer runway and simplified declaration options are the most direct benefit. For the EU's environmental credibility internationally, repeated postponements — even without substantive rollback — carry their own reputational cost with trading partners already sceptical of the rule.
what_to_watch: >
  The relaunched Information System is scheduled to gain further functionality through the summer of 2026 ahead of the December application date — IT readiness, not political disagreement, has been the trigger for both previous delays, so this is the number one thing to track. Also worth watching: whether the unchanged country risk-benchmarking list (low/standard/high risk, published May 2025) becomes a flashpoint again once enforcement actually begins, and how producer countries and the United States respond now that the Commission has signalled the substantive framework will not move further.
