tldr: "Reworked after a negative Regulatory Scrutiny Board opinion, the Digital Networks Act tries to balance telecoms consolidation against consumer and national oversight."
where_things_stand: >
  The Digital Networks Act was originally due by the end of 2025 but slipped after the Regulatory Scrutiny Board issued a negative opinion on the draft, forcing the Commission to rework it before tabling. It now moves in parallel with the Digital Omnibus, as part of the same simplification push for the telecoms and connectivity sector.
what_is_at_stake: >
  The file sits at the intersection of two competing goals: consolidating a fragmented EU telecoms market to help operators compete with US and Asian players, and preserving the consumer protections and national regulatory oversight that member states built up under the current framework. Telecom operators want fewer, larger rules that ease cross-border investment. National regulators and smaller member states are more cautious about ceding oversight to Brussels.
why_it_matters: >
  For network operators, the Act could unlock the scale needed to fund next-generation infrastructure, an argument reinforced by the parallel State of the Digital Decade findings on network investment gaps. For smaller telecom players and national regulators, consolidation rules risk favouring the largest operators unless safeguards are built in. For citizens, the practical effect is likely to be indirect and slow, felt through investment and pricing rather than any single new right.
what_to_watch: >
  The reworked draft is expected to move once the Regulatory Scrutiny Board's concerns are addressed. Given the delay already suffered, timing will be a signal in itself: a fast reintroduction suggests the Commission has resolved the substantive objections, while further slippage would point to unresolved disagreement inside the College. Once tabled, expect early positioning from national telecom regulators before Parliament and Council begin substantive work.
