title: "EU Digital Omnibus: one file adopted, one still contested"
date: 2026-08-15
topic: Digital
tldr: "What the Commission tabled as a single 'Digital Omnibus' in November 2025 split into two files moving at very different speeds: the AI Omnibus is adopted and in force, while the GDPR-facing Data Omnibus is still stuck in Council negotiation."
where_things_stand: >
  The Commission tabled the Digital Omnibus package in November 2025, the centrepiece of its digital simplification agenda for the current mandate. It split into two separate legislative files. The AI Omnibus, amending the AI Act, moved fast because it had a hard deadline to beat: the AI Act's own 2 August 2026 date for high-risk-system obligations. Parliament and the Council reached a trilogue agreement in May 2026, and the Council gave final approval on 29 June 2026; the text was published in the Official Journal on 24 July and entered into force on 27 July 2026. The Data Omnibus, covering the GDPR, ePrivacy, the Data Act, NIS2 and DORA, has no equivalent deadline forcing it forward: it remains before the Council, which was still circulating compromise texts as of May 2026, with core cookie-consent provisions still missing from the working text.
what_is_at_stake: >
  The core tension is compliance cost against regulatory safeguard, and it plays out differently on each track. On the AI side, the adopted text pushes back high-risk obligations by 16 months for standalone (Annex III) systems, to December 2027, and by 12 months for AI embedded in regulated products, to August 2028 — though transparency obligations under Article 50 were not delayed and took effect on schedule. On the data side, the fight is still open: industry has pushed for the Data Omnibus because GDPR and cybersecurity-reporting obligations are landing faster than many companies can absorb them, while civil society and some MEPs read parts of the same proposal as a rollback of safeguards negotiated as such rather than as red tape.
why_it_matters: >
  For large tech companies, the AI Omnibus offers a settled and workable compliance calendar, though the practical benefit is smaller than the political noise around it suggested, since well-resourced players were already close to ready. For SMEs and AI developers with thinner compliance capacity, the deferral buys real time. For anyone whose data is processed under the GDPR or ePrivacy rules, the file that actually touches their rights — the Data Omnibus — is the one still unresolved, and its slower pace means less pressure to reach an outcome quickly, for better or worse depending on which reading of "simplification" one takes.
what_to_watch: >
  With the AI track settled, attention shifts to the Data Omnibus: watch the Cypriot Presidency's successive compromise texts for whether the cookie-consent and GDPR provisions industry wants make it back in, and whether Parliament's LIBE and IMCO committees, having just finished the AI file, take a similarly fast or a much more cautious approach to this one. The EDPB and EDPS have already issued a joint opinion flagging risks on the GDPR elements, a sign this track will draw more institutional pushback than the AI Omnibus did.
