## Political-institutional overview

The original SFDR, applicable since March 2021, became a de facto product-labelling system for financial products, the well-known "Article 6, 8 and 9" funds, despite never being designed for that purpose, which generated confusion over the minimum criteria for each category. The "SFDR 2.0" revision seeks to replace that accidental system with product categorisation based on explicit minimum criteria, Sustainable, Transition and ESG Basics, each requiring at least 70% of the portfolio to support the relevant strategy, and notably simplifies the existing disclosure framework, in particular by removing entity-level disclosures.

</div>

<div class="dsection dsection-actors" markdown="1">

## Map of Actors

**European Commission.** DG FISMA leads this dossier, within the sustainable-finance framework, alongside the Taxonomy and the CSRD.

**European Parliament.** The Committee on Economic and Monetary Affairs (ECON) is responsible. Rapporteur: Gerben-Jan Gerbrandy (Renew Europe, Netherlands). Shadow rapporteurs: Luděk Niedermayer (EPP, Czechia), Lara Wolters (S&D, Netherlands), Jaroslava Pokorná Jermanová (Patriots for Europe, Czechia), Denis Nesci (ECR, Italy), Kira Marie Peter-Hansen (Greens, Denmark), Martin Günther (The Left, Germany). The Environment Committee (ENVI) decided not to issue an opinion on this file.

**Council.** The Council closed its negotiating mandate on 24 June 2026, under the outgoing Cypriot Presidency, just before the Irish Presidency took over on 1 July. The Council's text broadly welcomes the Commission's proposal, preserving the new product-categorisation framework while proposing targeted calibrations, notably mandatory principal-adverse-impact (PAI) indicators for the Sustainable and Transition categories and a longer, 24-month application window.

**External stakeholders.** Eurosif, the European sustainable finance association, has published analysis favourable to the process, with reservations about the need for clear and robust product criteria. Finance Watch views the proposal positively but calls for stronger rule clarity to prevent greenwashing.

<p class="to-verify">Formal positions from asset managers and industry associations specifically on the Transition category's treatment of fossil-fuel exposure, now that this has emerged as the central point of contention.</p>

## Advanced political analysis

<div class="read-card">
<div class="read-card-head"><span class="read-card-num">01</span><span class="read-card-title">A wider ideological spread than on the CSDDD or the CSRD</span></div>
<p>The shadow-rapporteur line-up runs from the EPP to the Left, with a Patriots for Europe shadow in between, a broader political spread than on the CSDDD or the CSRD. More than 600 amendments tabled on the draft report is a direct indicator of that fragmentation, and a plausible reason the ECON vote has already slipped once.</p>
</div>

<div class="read-card">
<div class="read-card-head"><span class="read-card-num">02</span><span class="read-card-title">The real fight is fossil fuel, not the categories themselves</span></div>
<p>Institutions broadly agree on moving from Articles 8/9 to explicit product categories. Where they remain divided, according to firms tracking the file closely, is how far the new "Transition" category should accommodate fossil-fuel companies pursuing a credible transition strategy. That single question may do more to shape the final text than any of the more technical disclosure mechanics.</p>
</div>

<div class="read-card">
<div class="read-card-head"><span class="read-card-num">03</span><span class="read-card-title">The Council has less room to move than it looks</span></div>
<p>Having closed its mandate before the Presidency handover, the Council enters trilogue with a settled position built around 24-month implementation and mandatory PAI indicators. That gives Parliament genuine leverage on the fossil-fuel question, since the Council will be negotiating from a fixed text rather than a flexible one.</p>
</div>

<div class="dsection dsection-transposition" markdown="1">

## Transposition by country

Not applicable in the way it would be for a directive: as a Regulation, the revised SFDR will be directly applicable across all member states once adopted, without national transposition.
