## Political-institutional overview

Presented as a Council Directive amending six core direct-tax instruments — including the Interest and Royalty Directive, the Parent-Subsidiary Directive (renamed to reflect a broadened scope covering dividends and other profit distributions), the Merger Directive, the ATAD interest-limitation rules, and the FASTER withholding-tax relief framework — the Omnibus's centrepiece is the abolition of withholding taxes on cross-border dividend, interest and royalty payments between EU companies. It also makes the 30% EBITDA interest-limitation threshold mandatory across all Member States, removing the discretion some currently have to set a stricter cap, and expands the list of company forms and cross-border restructuring operations eligible for tax-neutral treatment.

Because tax measures fall under Article 115 TFEU, the European Parliament is only consulted — its opinion is non-binding — while the Council must act by unanimity. That is a structurally different, and typically far slower, procedure than the ordinary legislative files covered elsewhere on this site (the CSRD, the PPWR, the Digital Networks Act), where Parliament and Council are co-legislators on equal footing. The Commission's estimated €7.9 billion in annual savings is comparable in scale to other 2026 omnibus packages, but unanimity means a single Member State can block or dilute the outcome.

## Advanced political analysis

This file is a useful contrast case with the other 2026 omnibus dossiers tracked on this site. Substantively, it shares the same "simplification versus erosion of safeguards" framing: the mandatory 30% EBITDA threshold removes Member State discretion to set stricter interest-deduction limits, and loosening the withholding-tax and minimum-holding-period requirements has a direct anti-avoidance dimension, not merely an administrative one. Procedurally, though, it is far more exposed to being watered down or stalled indefinitely, since unanimity in the Council gives any single Member State an effective veto — the same dynamic that has repeatedly stalled EU direct-tax harmonisation efforts over the past two decades.

## Impact by sector

**Multinational groups with intra-EU cross-border structures** are the direct addressees: the abolition of withholding tax on intra-EU dividends, interest and royalties, and the broadened Merger Directive scope (covering simplified mergers and divisions by separation, and tax-neutral cross-border conversions), reduce friction for corporate reorganisations. **Holding-company jurisdictions** face a more searching question, since part of their competitive position today rests on the very variation in withholding-tax and holding-period rules the Omnibus proposes to flatten.

<div class="dsection dsection-transposition" markdown="1">

## Transposition by country

As a Directive rather than a Regulation — unlike the PPWR or the Digital Networks Act covered elsewhere on this site — the Taxation Omnibus would require national transposition once adopted, on a timeline not yet set given the file has not reached Council agreement. Proposed application is from 1 January 2029 at the earliest, "with certain exceptions."
