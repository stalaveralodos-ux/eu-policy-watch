## Political-institutional overview

Unlike the CSDDD and the CSRD, the Taxonomy's simplification has run mainly through delegated acts rather than a codecided directive. That means Parliament and Council have had only a scrutiny role, the ability to object within a fixed window, rather than line-by-line negotiation, which has made the process noticeably faster than the CSDDD or the CSRD.

The most significant change from the first wave is a materiality threshold: non-financial companies can exclude economic activities from Taxonomy eligibility and alignment assessment when they cumulatively represent less than 10% of the entity's turnover, CapEx or OpEx. Reporting templates and the "do no significant harm" (DNSH) pollution criteria were also simplified, and mandatory reporting scope narrowed to companies with more than €450 million in turnover and over 1,000 employees.

The Commission has also launched a more systematic review of the technical screening criteria and reporting requirements themselves, published for consultation in March 2026, still without confirmed adoption as of mid-August 2026.

## Advanced political analysis

<div class="read-card">
<div class="read-card-head"><span class="read-card-num">01</span><span class="read-card-title">Delegated acts trade speed for amendment power</span></div>
<p>Using a delegated act instead of a directive lets the Commission move faster, but it also limits Parliament and Council's room for amendment to a simple binary veto within the scrutiny window. That is why the Taxonomy's simplification has advanced faster than the CSRD's or the CSDDD's, even though all three are part of the same Omnibus I package announced in February 2025.</p>
</div>

<div class="read-card">
<div class="read-card-head"><span class="read-card-num">02</span><span class="read-card-title">The second wave has already slipped once</span></div>
<p>The broader technical screening criteria review was targeted for adoption in mid-June 2026. As of mid-August, that has not been confirmed. Delegated acts are supposed to be the fast track; a missed internal target here is a small but real sign that even the lighter-touch instrument is not immune to the same delivery pressure seen on files like the PPWR.</p>
</div>

<div class="read-card">
<div class="read-card-head"><span class="read-card-num">03</span><span class="read-card-title">"Clarification, not policy change" is a framing to watch</span></div>
<p>The Commission presents the second wave as making criteria clearer and more workable, not reopening core policy choices. Whether that framing survives contact with the final text, particularly on DNSH criteria, which touch most economic activities, is the more interesting question than the review's technical content itself.</p>
</div>
