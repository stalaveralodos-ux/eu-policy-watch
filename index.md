## Political-institutional overview

The DNA follows the same institutional logic as the PPWR: it replaces a directive-based regime — the European Electronic Communications Code (EECC) — with a single, directly applicable Regulation, ending the possibility of divergent national transposition. It also consolidates the BEREC Regulation, core parts of the Open Internet Regulation, elements of the ePrivacy Directive and the Radio Spectrum Policy Programme into one instrument, and creates a new EU body, the Office for Digital Networks (ODN), which absorbs the functions of the former BEREC Office.

The centrepiece is a "single passport" authorisation system: a provider wishing to operate in more than one member state would submit a single notification to one national regulator, which forwards it through the ODN to the other member states concerned. A parallel EU-level authorisation is introduced for satellite spectrum. The proposal reached publication only after the Commission reworked it following a negative Regulatory Scrutiny Board opinion on the draft impact assessment in late October 2025 — a delay that itself signals the political sensitivity of ceding national regulatory competence in this area.

## Map of Actors

**European Commission.** DG CNECT leads the dossier, framing it as necessary to fund next-generation 5G/6G and fibre infrastructure and to help EU operators compete at scale with US and Asian players.

**European Parliament.** The file sits in ITRE. Michał Kobosko (Renew, Poland) holds the rapporteurship — a notable outcome given the EPP did not secure it, leaving Pilar del Castillo Vera (EPP, Spain) in a shadow role. S&D shadows Elena Sancho Murillo and Matthias Ecke, and ECR's Alessandro Torselli and Susanna Solier, are among the MEPs positioning on the file.

**Council.** The six-state intervention of December 2025 (Austria, France, Germany, Hungary, Italy, Slovenia) set the tone for Council scrutiny: preserving national control over spectrum and licensing is the recurring concern raised in working-group discussions through mid-2026.

## Advanced political analysis

The dossier sits at the intersection of two competing EU objectives: consolidating a fragmented telecoms market to unlock investment at scale, and preserving the national regulatory oversight and consumer protections built up under the EECC. The pre-publication pushback from six member states — before the text was even public — is unusual and suggests the Commission will need to make real concessions on spectrum governance to secure a Council mandate, rather than treating national resistance as a negotiating formality.

## Impact by sector

**Telecom operators.** A single EU passport and harmonised authorisation could unlock cross-border investment and consolidation, long sought by large operators. **National regulators.** The transfer of coordination functions to the new ODN changes the practical role of NRAs and BEREC. **Satellite and cloud providers.** EU-level spectrum authorisation brings satellite connectivity fully into the harmonised framework for the first time.

## Transposition by country

Not applicable in the traditional sense: as a Regulation, the DNA does not require national transposition. What is relevant to track is how national regulatory authorities reorganise as coordination functions shift toward the ODN, and how member states respond to the loss of "gold-plating" discretion on authorisation conditions.
