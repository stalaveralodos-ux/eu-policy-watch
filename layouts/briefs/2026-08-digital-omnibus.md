**Where things stand**

The Commission tabled the Digital Omnibus in November 2025, the centrepiece of its digital simplification agenda for the current mandate. It does not create new rules. It reopens parts of the existing digital rulebook, including elements of the AI Act and the data governance framework, to reduce implementation burden. Parliament and Council are expected to reach a general position by mid-2026, with full implementation realistically pushed into 2027. The parallel Digital Networks Act has already slipped once, after a negative opinion from the Regulatory Scrutiny Board delayed its draft.

**What is at stake**

The core tension is compliance cost against regulatory safeguard. Industry has pushed for the Omnibus precisely because AI Act obligations are landing faster than many companies can absorb them. Civil society and some MEPs read the same move as a rollback dressed as simplification, opening provisions that were negotiated as safeguards rather than red tape. Both readings can be true of the same clause, which is what makes the file contested rather than technical.

**Why it matters**

For large tech companies, the Omnibus offers a chance to renegotiate obligations they are already resourced to meet, so the practical benefit is smaller than the political noise suggests. For SMEs and AI developers with thinner compliance capacity, timing matters more than substance: a rulebook that shifts again before it has stabilised is its own cost, regardless of which direction it moves. For users, the file rarely translates into anything visible unless a safeguard is removed outright, which is the scenario campaigners are most focused on flagging.

**What to watch**

Parliament is split between those who see the Omnibus as too timid and those looking for grounds to block it, so the text is likely to keep moving before it settles. Council's general position, expected around mid-2026, will show which provisions face the most resistance among Member States. Industry positioning will sharpen once trilogues are in view, since early engagement has more room to shape drafting than intervention after positions harden. The State of the Digital Decade package, running in parallel, keeps sovereignty and infrastructure investment high on the agenda and gives industry a stronger argument for predictability over further simplification.
